## Terminology
The set of terms and their definitions that are agreed upon, allowing flawless communication and minimising ambiguity.

## Two Traits (of Identity)
The foundational components of identity:
Group affiliation (“Who are my people?”)
Territorial association (“Where do we collectively operate and take responsibility?”)
Historically coupled through evolution. [Read more.](/essays/identity.md)

## Cognitive Dissonance
A psychological state of discomfort that arises when an individual's conflicting beliefs, attitudes, or behaviors interact closely within context. This leads to compulsion/motivation to reduce the inconsistency by changing beliefs, justifying actions, or altering behavior.

+Extension— Dissonance arising from tension between evolutionary biases/behaviour due to conflicting social constraints. Lack of dissonance when low interaction between conflicting beliefs (non-contextual beliefs).

## Belief Break
A threshold event in which sustained contradiction between beliefs and perceived reality renders that belief model or value unreliable, causing low confidence in that context and triggering accelerated learning but often noisy.

## Mental Injury
A disruption to psychological stability that impair decision-making, emotional regulation, perception/salience. Caused by belief-break, cognitive dissonance.

## Environmental Feedback
Signals (social, economic, or physical) that reinforce or penalize behavior, shaping learning, beliefs, and habit formation.

## Behavioral Model
A full set of habits, beliefs, and response patterns to environmental stimuli of an individual. This is a state snapshot at a particular point in time and is subject to evolving.

## Salience
Certain information, stimuli, or thoughts stand out and capture attention due to their specific properties, influencing perception, memory retention and decision-making.

## Empirical Truth/Factual Truth
Empirical truth can be verified using observations through scientific methods.

## Social Truth
Information that gains behavioral authority through widespread social referencing and reinforcement. Regardless of its factual accuracy, it is considered in behaviour and decision-making, since it’s part of the belief system of a significant portion of the population.

## Social Node
Within a social network/graph, a node is an informationally interactive entity. The rank of a node represents its influence based on social referencing. This includes elements of an information system, such as individuals or organisations, mass media, etc.

## Social Referencing
Information, behaviors, opinions, or reactions from other social nodes that are used as evidence by individuals for interpreting situations, forming beliefs, or deciding actions.

## Watchdog
An individual, group, or institution that monitors the actions of others to ensure accountability, transparency, and compliance with rules or standards, to prevent abusive and exploitative behaviour.

## Power Structure
The organized system of individuals, groups, and institutions that hold authority and influence within a society or organization, shaping decisions, resource distribution, and behavioral norms.

Formal: Systemically accepted laws and constitutions.

Informal: See [Shadow Hierarchy.](/appendices/glossary.md#shadow-hierarchy)

## Shadow Hierarchy
Social hierarchies, networks with significant influence power (economic, political, social) but no public visibility. In short, hidden power structures.

## Large populations/Everyday people
A broad group of individuals considered collectively, often used to describe patterns, trends, or behaviors at scale. At the individual scale, they do not possess specialized expertise, authority, or exceptional circumstances.

## Abstract Warfare
Conflict that does not occur through direct physical violence but operates through other modes, such as

- Economic warfare
- Psychological warfare
  - Identity warfare
  - Information warfare
- Cyber warfare

These modes do not operate in isolation but overlap in practical implementation.

## PsyOps (Psychological Operations)
Planned activities that manipulate information and the environment to exploit psychology, influencing sentiment, attitude, and behaviors in support of broader objectives. Targets range from a large subset of the population to an individual.

## Identity Wiring
Identity wiring refers to the structured set of sentiments and opinions of various identities towards each other and the self, driven by human biases. Formed by [information system](#information-system). This heavily influences further/ongoing competition and warfare of any mode and co-evolves with it.

## Identity Fishing
A PsyOp tactic that propagates intricately designed information through various media targeted towards specific identity(ies). Although information accessible publicly as neutral, its perception by targeted identities is driven by high salience towards self two-traits and pre-trained beliefs. Effects include reinforcing/altering identity wiring, distorting perceived identity population distribution.

## Information System
An Information system is an ecosystem of components (people, data/information, technologies) capable of performing the following functions in a specific context.

- Collect data
- Store data
- Process data
- Distribute data
- Generate new information/data

## Super Structure
A socio-technical system that integrates human behavior, information flows, physical & abstract structures, and their co-evolution into a single analytical unit.
