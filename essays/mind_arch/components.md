### Cognitive lens

This comprises evolutionary traits (reward system, emotions, cognitive biases) and learned data collectively affecting incoming information before it is encoded into memory.

Salience/filtering: Our senses continuously get bombarded with information. A lot more than we can consciously handle. But the cognitive lens automatically filters out most of it and keeps focus on relevant pieces of information. This relevance is a combination of evolutionary traits and learned information/behaviour.

Framing: Existing learned data is retrieved based on the current context, incoming information, subjective salience, and prior emotional experiences.


### Emotions:

Works as a compass to navigate the environment, e.g., fearful response to threatening situations and fleeing or forming relationship bonds. Real-time response to environmental stimuli while recalling memories.

Even though emotions are evolutionary traits, how we manage them internally and respond is a learned skill.

Experiences are emotionally tagged and stored in memory. We can recall memories based on emotions such as the most fun or the most fearful, etc.

Emotional response is also normalised to our experiences. Remember any childhood events like exams that felt tense at that time, but when we look back, they seem tiny compared to the challenges we face as adults.

### Memory store.

Storage of all memories and experiences. Made up of data and intra-data associations, forming a web of information.

e.g., think about the term “pet” and you’ll start gathering all related information that you have, like pets you had or kinds of pets people have, etc.

Weaker data associations cause an inability to recall the information despite the existence of information in the mind. This happens to rarely accessed information. We tend to operate smoothly in the context of regular work since frequent access strengthens the associations in that context.

Encoding does not happen according to the chronological order of events that occurred, but the order in which information is received or accessed.

If you have heavily researched an event that occurred decades ago, you are likely to consider it more than an event that happened a year ago and is more relevant but you haven’t used it since then.

Although most animals gather information, trying to keep conscious reality as close as possible to objective reality for accurate navigation, two realities really are distinct. Conscious reality is a separate copy of objective reality. And cognitive biases perform heuristic scans on this copy (conscious reality/memory store), which may lead to miscalculation.

### Working memory

Compared to a seemingly infinite memory store, working memory is finite. This is similar to the RAM, where data is retrieved according to the current context of work, and information is made available at the fingertips for fast access. You must have experienced how it takes time and active thinking to get up to speed for deep research work. This is also why it feels frictional and energy costly to move between multiple contexts of work, especially when contexts are semantically distant.

Sometimes, early-day emotional experiences, whether good or bad, set the tone for the whole day, which affects thinking and behaviour; that emotional state is maintained here. Since information and memories are also retrieved through associations of negative or positive emotions, we also tend to retrieve more of the same. This can affect decision-making and hinder critical thinking.

Thinking on retrieved information and memories also encodes back to the memory store (alters, strengthens). e.g., Only thinking about positive or negative aspects of a specific entity leads to the respective association with that entity.

### Attention mechanism

Responsible for bringing our attention to unfinished/pending tasks, commitments, habits, and other high-priority actions.

Ever feel the need to set a reminder for checking social media, playing games, watching favorite shows, or eating unfinished favorite food in the kitchen? The attention mechanism sends those push notifications.

If you feel like the cup is filling despite trying to empty it, this may be the cause.

When thinking about a “second brain,” the attention mechanism is one of the few cognitive processes that benefits from being offloaded. A simple, externally maintained and prioritised to-do list can significantly improve focus.

### Error management

Identifies discrepancies between various pieces of information and keeps the behaviour model consistent and avoids the formation of mismatched beliefs. e.g. If you are discussing with somebody and that person says something that contradicts earlier statements, you are likely to catch that.

But despite this, a high quantity of information in the modern environment malforms the cognitive model. Especially, in early life, where cognitive abilities such as critical thinking are still underdeveloped, the information and environmental feedback for years is absorbed and encoded with little or no manual arrangement. This hinders subsequent perception and error management, forming an inconsistent behavioural model. e.g. holding non-aligned or contradictory beliefs due to high social reference.

Sustained error in any specific context/skill causes a belief break, putting us on our toes, reducing our confidence, and endorsing rethinking to reduce the error margin.

Cognitive dissonance: A psychological state of discomfort that arises when an individual holds conflicting beliefs, attitudes, or behaviours. This leads to compulsion/motivation to reduce the inconsistency by changing beliefs, justifying actions, or altering behaviour. e.g Doing something that parents/family told not to do. This also arises due to a mismatch between evolutionary traits and behaviour.

You must have realised by now that what we call conscience is a product of this. It is not a super tool to identify what is objectively right, but it is only capable of identifying a mismatch between various pieces of information/behaviour (various combinations of learned and evolutionary traits/biases). i.e. works within the context of conscious reality and not objective reality.

e.g.

Deviating thinking and actions from social norms causes friction, even if an individual is objectively correct.

When identifying a mating partner, sexual attraction is primarily driven by physical and behavioural feature recognition of the opposite sex. But non-shared identity variables may disturb the pair bonding. Both are evolutionary biases but likely to cause cognitive dissonance.

Error management should be actively considered for enculturing human behaviour that aligns with sustainability while also minimising mental stress (per capita kind).

## Processes

We looked at components and their function in a somewhat isolated manner. Now we’ll look at few processes that occur throughout our daily activities, comprising multiple components.

### Perception (Information Inflow)

We start gathering information and learning from the environment since birth. In the earliest memories we can remember, we are already capable of speech. Our cognitive model actively develops from environmental information in a continuous, recurrent process throughout the whole life.

As we saw earlier, perception of incoming information involves a combination of various components that collectively form cognitive lenses. As we gather more and more information, the factor of learned information increases relative to evolutionary traits. Learned information also includes information about human psychology, such as emotions, reward system, and identity, etc., potentially decreasing their negative impact on critical thinking.

Mental injuries such as identity crises or highly emotionally valent experiences destabilise cognitive lenses.

### Meditating on existing information/ Active Thinking

As seen in C1, contemplating existing memories and data will lead to a similar response from evolutionary components C2. e.g. Feeling associated emotions when thinking about past memories.

In this case, critical thinking can be more effective due to the absence of environmental constraints, but is also limited to known information.

Contemplating over specific information driven by evolutionary salience (emotionally stimulating or rewarding) increases its effect on subsequent perception, even more than necessary. Considering this, active information gathering and thinking from multiple sources with diverse backgrounds are important to avoid fallacies of bias.

### Information outflow, actions

This includes talking, using motor skills to perform actions, play sports etc. This mostly occurs concurrently with perception. e.g while conversing, we hear what the other is saying, we set the context for dialogue and retrieve existing memories and prepare information to talk back.

## Information sources
- DNA - Evolutionary traits, including cognitive biases, emotions, reward system come from this.
- Environment - Enculturation. Learned behaviour, like languages, cultural beliefs, and work skills, is acquired after we are born.
- Self-Generated/Reflective - This is relatively rare and a product of active thinking on observed data, forming new ideas, meaningful combinations, series of personal choices. e.g. artists creating paintings, music, and lyrics to name a few.
